**High-Throughput Proteomics Sample Preparation Using Magnetic Beads on the KingFisher System**

Created by: Paloma Lopez and Dawn Moran

Modified: Paloma Lopez, Annie Stefanides, Rob Lampe 2025-2026

**Introduction:**

This protocol describes a magnetic bead–based protein extraction and clean-up workflow optimized for environmental seawater biomass and cultured samples. It is adapted from Hughes et al. (2014, 2019) and Cytiva Sera-Mag SpeedBeads protocols and incorporates automation using the KingFisher Flex system.

**Equipment/supplies:**

- Eppendorf Centrifuge (Minispin Plus or 5810 R)

- Savant DNA120 SpeedVac concentrator

- Eppendorf Thermomixer R

- 2.0 ml Low adhesion microcentrifuge PP tubes with cap (ethanol washed; 1420-2600)

- 96 deep-well tray

- Silicon cover for 96 deep well plate

Additional supplies for filtered environmental samples:

- Luer-slip 5 ml plastic syringe (S7510-5)

- Syringe filter 5 um low protein binding Millex-SV (SLSV025LS)

- 15 ml Falcon tube

- Vivaspin 6 Membrane: 5,000 MWCO PES (<span class="mark">VS0611)</span>

**Reagents:**

- Water, Optima LC/MS Grade (W6)

- Lysis buffer: 50mM HEPES pH 8.5 (Boston BioProducts \#BB-2082)/2% SDS

- Benzonase Nuclease (Novagen 70746-3)

- 200mM DTT in 50mM HEPES pH 8.5

- 400mM iodoacetamide in 50mM HEPES pH 8.5

- Magnetic beads at 20µg/µl ([<u>see protocol for making beads solution</u>](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1CXA960OZLp_abgrQsnWfvsg5gAib3BHbV4_yK2BTBXA/edit?usp=sharing))

- 100% ethanol (HPLC/Spectrophotometric grade 200 proof, Sigma-Aldrich \#459828)

- 100% acetonitrile

- 80% ethanol

- 0.05 M HEPES pH 8.0 (Boston BioProducts \#BB-2080)

- Trypsin in 0.05 M HEPES pH 8.0 at 0.5µg/µl

- 2% DMSO

- 1% formic acid (for cultured samples)

Notes: All solutions are made in LC/MS grade water (Optima W64). For instructions on stock reagents’ preparations click on the [<u>document link</u>](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/19EXv9J5pyjlpAfcDir9E9ICdOfBgafUizRDmBHi_MIA/edit?usp=sharing).

**Procedure:**

1\. To extract the biomass from the sample:

> **If sample is an environmental marine (complex) sample:**

1)  Extract biomass from filter with lysis buffer (2% SDS, 50mM HEPES pH 8.5). Lysis buffer can be heated to 37℃ if SDS has precipitated out.

    1)  Use tweezers to unfold filter and bend it out to expose the surface area where the biomass is at.

    2)  Submerge filters into a 15 mL Falcon tube containing enough buffer to make sure the filter is fully covered by the buffer (~4 mL for ½ of a 142mm filter).

    3)  Heat at 95℃ for 12 min (do not go over time).

    4)  Incubate at RT for 30 min-1hr at 350 RPM.

    5)  Remove filter from lysed sample (drain well before removing).

    6)  Filter solution using a syringe and 5 um syringe filter.

    7)  Rinse syringe/filter by filtering an additional 500 uL of lysis buffer.

    8)  Centrifuge tubes at maximum speed possible (4000 rpm if big rotor or 9000 rpm if small rotor) for 20-30 min at RT.

2)  Rinse 5,000 MWCO Vivaspin tubes with about 900 uL of lysis buffer to remove tube contaminants. Centrifuge lysing buffer for at least 30 minutes. Discard any lysing buffer left on top and bottom of tube.

3)  Pour supernatant (no pellet) into a rinsed 5,000 MWCO Vivaspin tube and concentrate to ~400 µL at:

    1)  if big rotor: 4,000 rpm and 30℃

    2)  if small rotor: 7,000 rpm and 30℃

4)  Rinse solution by adding 500 uL of lysis buffer to concentrate. Concentrate back down to ~400 µL centrifuging for approx. 20 min.

    1)  If using 5 mL vivaspin: dump excess liquid from bottom section of the tube before adding the buffer and spinning again (make sure you are dumping the excess liquid from the bottom not the concentrated portion in the top section of the tube)

5)  Measure sample volume while transferring it to an ethanol washed 2 mL cryovial.

    1)  Final concentrated volume will vary depending on the amount of biomass present in the filter. Lower biomass samples can get concentrated down to 150-200 uL while higher concentrated samples can get concentrated to 500-600 uL

**If sample is from a culture:**

1)  Take about 2 mL (for a healthy culture) and centrifuge for 20 minutes at 14,100 x rpm in an ethanol washed 2 mL low adhesion microcentrifuge tube.

2)  Remove supernatant (could save if working with low-availability samples in case extraction has low yields).

3)  Add 150-500 uL of lysis buffer (2% SDS, 50mM HEPES pH 8.5) based on the size of the pellet. Make sure pellet is fully resuspended. Lysis buffer might need to be heated to 37C to be homogenous.

    1)  If pellet is too big and is not resuspending fully, add more buffer in batches of 50 uL until full resuspension.

4)  Heat at 95C for 10 min (do not go over time).

5)  Incubate at RT for 30 min-1hr at 350 RPM.

6)  Centrifuge tubes at (14000 rpm) for 20-30 min at RT to remove cell debris.

7)  Remove supernatant into a new ethanol washed tube. Proteins should be dissolved in solution at this point.

    1)  Could keep pellets if looking for a specific protein, otherwise, toss pellets.

*\*\*\*\*Could freeze at -20℃ at this point to continue the next day. Thaw samples at RT or at 37℃\*\*\*\**

2\. Quantify total protein in sample solution ([<u>see protocol</u>](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1DiSqbPVc_rl61QsMEG68wQeIWxrTidawtnJHJwtLNaU/edit?tab=t.0)).

3\. Subsample **60 ug** of protein from the lysate solution. Add enough lysing buffer to bring solution up to **300 ul** (for a 0.2 ug/uL protein concentration).

- If the solution is too diluted

- The volumes for the reagents used are dependent on the initial sample volume. Make sure to adjust if the initial volume changes.

- Archive the rest of the lysate as a backup at -80℃.

*\*\*\*\*Could freeze at -20℃ at this point to continue the next day. Thaw samples at RT or at 37℃\*\*\*\**

4\. Add **2 µl** (50 units) of benzonase nuclease to degrade chromatin (excess intact-chromatin will disrupt SP3). Incubate for 30-40 minutes at 37℃.

5\. Add **15 µl** of 200 mM DTT in 50 mM HEPES pH 8.5 (5µl of DTT per 100 µl of lysate) in the fume hood. Incubate at 45℃ for 30 minutes. This step reduces disulfide bonds.

6\. Add **30 µl** of 400 mM iodoacetamide in 50 mM HEPES pH 8.5 (10µl of iodoacetamide per 100 µl of lysate) in the fume hood. Incubate in dark for 30 minutes at 24℃. This step achieves alkylation of sulfhydryl groups.

7\. Add **30 µl** of 200 mM DTT in 50 mM HEPES pH 8.5 (10 µl DTT per 100 µl of lysate). This step quenches unreacted iodoacetamide. Incubate at RT for at least 10 minutes.

*\*\*\*\*Could freeze at -20℃ at this point to continue the next day. Thaw samples at RT or at 37℃\*\*\*\**

**Protein Clean-up**

8\. Add **30 µl** of 20 µg/µl magnetic beads for a 10:1 ratio (600 µg beads/60ug of protein).

*\*\*\*\*Do not freeze the sample with the beads at any point\*\*\*\**

9\. Immediately add **407 uL** of 100% ethanol to promote protein binding to the beads for a final 50% ethanol solution

10\. Mix sample solution either by pipetting in and out or by vortexing while preventing beads sticking to tube walls.

11\. Incubate for 15 minutes at RT shaking at 650 rpm. Centrifuge if needed after for two seconds and at no more than 1000 rpm.

*\*\*\*\*From hereon, samples are transferred from 2 mL tubes to 96 deep-well trays. KF replaces magnetic racks\*\**

12\. Transfer samples from 2 mL eppendorf tubes into 96 deep-well trays by pipetting samples into each well. Record in lab notebook tray location of samples (rack is labeled A-H, 1-12). An adjustable multichannel pipette is available for this.

13\. According to how many sample wells you have filled in your tray, make up each of the reagent 96 deep-well trays, a multichannel micropipette is available for this step:

- Starting Sample: Your samples transferred from eppendorf tubes

- Wash (Ethanol_1): **700 uL** of 80% EtOH

- Repeat Wash (Ethanol_2): **700 uL** of 80% EtOH

- Binding (Acetonitrile): **700 uL** of 100% ACN

- Elution: **90 uL** of 100mM ammonium bicarbonate or 50 mM HEPES pH 8.0

14\. Connect laptop to KF and turn on. Open up BindIt4.1 software

- Make sure the KF is connected by looking at the bottom of the window

- If not connected → press connect → select 711-85562 → screen on KF should say “Computer control” when connected

15\. In the “Home” tab, select “**ProteinCleanUp_v2.bdz**” from under recent protocols, or click the “open” widget and select the correct protocol

- Press start → The software will now prompt you to save the run report. Save this report to the folder Desktop → Bindit 4.1 → Report Files → click “Go”

16\. The KF will direct you on the instrument screen which plates to load. Follow the instructions (ie load tip comb, load sample plate, etc.) for loading and when done with each, press “start” to continue

- Load boxes with correct orientation → look for A1 corner

17\. After loading the last tray, the program will start running each step. Watch the protocol run periodically

18\. Quantify the purified protein using the BSA assay

19\. Calculate the amount of trypsin to add based on the purified protein values for each sample at a 1:20 trypsin to protein ratio.

20\. Once the KF is done, **add trypsin** 0.5ug/ul in 100mM ammonium bicarbonate or 50mM Hepes pH 8.0.

21\. Cover the sample tray with silicone square-well tray lids and place plate in thermomixer heat block for 14 hr/overnight digestion at 37°C while mixing at 400rpm

*\*\*do not ever freeze beads\*\**

**Peptide Recovery and Preparation with Kingfisher Flex**

22\. After overnight incubation at 37°C remove the plate from the heat block and spin down plate in tray centrifuge adapter for 20 seconds @ 1000 rpm to bring condensation down from lid (no higher than this speed, don’t want to damage beads)

23\. Remove old beads by placing the plate in the KF and running the program BeadRemoval.bdz. For that program you will need:

- A new comb in a comb plate

- Sample plate

24\. Add **20 uL** of new bead solution at 20 ug/uL to each sample well. Shake plate after adding beads to fully re-suspend beads into solution

25\. Add **1790 uL** of 100% acetonitrile to each well for a ~94% final acetonitrile concentration. Ideal concentration should be 95% or higher but we are limited by deep well plate max volume. Mix beads into solution after addition of acetonitrile by pipetting in and out. Make sure to scrape the bottom and the walls of the plate to release beads.

26\. Split solution into two plates by separating half of the total volume (950 uL) into the same positions in a new plate. Max volume on each 96 well plate to not overflow with the comb submerged is 950 uL.

27\. Cover the plate and incubate for 20 mins on the thermomixer with ~450-550 rpm shaking at RT.

28\. Prepare following solutions for the next KF step:

- Wash (Acetonitrile): **700 uL** of 100% ACN- deep well

- DMSO_Elution: **90 uL** of 2% DMSO- shallow well

29\. Once all of your plates are prepared and ready, load up “PeptideRecovery_Round1_v3.bdz” protocol on Bindit4.1 software from recent protocols, or click the “open” widget and select the correct protocol

30\. The KF will direct you on the instrument screen which plates to load. Follow the instructions (ie load tip comb, load sample plate, etc.) for loading and when done with each, press “start” to continue. At this point, you will have two identical plates containing half of your sample each, which the KF protocol will combine

31\. The software will now prompt you to save the run report. Save this report to the folder Desktop → Bindit 4.1 → Report Files

32\. Start the protocol

30\. Once the protocol is done, grab the DMSO elution plate and make sure you have new 1.5 mL EtOH rinsed tubes with corresponding samples labeled. With the plate on the Opentrons magnetic block, very carefully pipette out eluant (should be ~90uL) into a new 1.5 mL EtOH rinsed tube. At this point all of the beads should be taken out by the KF magnet during the protocol and no beads should be left in solution

31\. Place the 1.5 mL tube (EtOH rinsed) into the magnetic rack and let beads aggregate to magnet for 5 mins. Ensure no beads have been recovered, and if so repeat transfer once more.

32\. Add **10 uL** of 1% TFA (first step of zip tipping) before freezing if you are storing the sample before zip tipping.

*\*\*\*\*Could freeze at -20℃ at this point to continue the next day.\*\*\*\**

35\. Proceed to [<u>manual Zip Tipping Protocol</u>](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1rgApe9iVtNiBe-OQ_LaCX6LcSvJwXS-9MwJxFl5Ql7Y/edit#heading=h.dtuvoghgwca9) or [<u>automated Zip Tipping Protocol</u>](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1uP2y4rS4FjTO6As5prH0YPSg-sq1rWGwpVYoK3yWq_Y/edit?tab=t.0#heading=h.do6o91h5ks0m)

36\. Store samples at -20C for MS analysis or at -80C for long term storage
